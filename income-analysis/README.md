# 📊 The Income Equation: How Life Factors Add Up

**Course Project | STAT 311 | R, Tidyverse**

Analyzed General Social Survey (GSS) data from 1972–2022 to investigate how education, race, happiness, age, health, and marital satisfaction relate to household income.

### What I Did
- Cleaned and analyzed survey data to explore relationships between demographic and lifestyle factors and household income.
- Built and compared four linear regression models using training and testing datasets.
- Used bootstrapping to estimate confidence intervals for the relationships between income, education, and age.
- Created visualizations to examine income distributions, correlations, and differences across demographic and happiness groups.

### Tools & Methods
- **R & Tidyverse:** Data cleaning, transformation, and analysis
- **ggplot2:** Data visualization and exploratory data analysis
- **Statistical modeling:** Multiple linear regression, bootstrapping, confidence intervals, and correlation analysis
- **Model evaluation:** Adjusted R² and test-set RMSE

### Key Findings
- The education and general happiness model performed best among the four models compared.
- Each additional year of education was associated with approximately $5,775 higher predicted household income, holding happiness constant.
- Respondents reporting higher happiness levels tended to have higher predicted household incomes after accounting for education.
